Small motions from apps I use every day, and what each one tells you.

## the bar in Linear's sidebar toggle

The toggle draws the layout, and its bar is the sidebar. When the sidebar
closes, the bar slides to the edge on the same spring, so one click teaches
you the button.

<SidebarDemo />

## the next menu opens without a click

In the macOS menu bar the first menu needs a click. After that, each title opens
as the pointer reaches it. Toolbar tooltips do the same: the first waits, the
rest appear at once.

<MenuDemo />

## the switch stretches before it moves

Hold an iOS switch and the thumb widens toward where it will go. The value
changes when you let go, so you can still back out.

<SwitchDemo />

## all three window buttons answer together

Point at one macOS window button and all three show their glyphs, so you read
every choice before you pick.

<WindowDemo />

## one dropdown that moves between menus

In Stripe's navigation, one panel slides and resizes between items instead of
closing and reopening. Your eye follows one surface.

<DropdownDemo />

## the progress bar grows on hover

On YouTube and Spotify the bar is a thin line while you watch. Reach for it and
it thickens and grows a knob, a bigger target when you need one.

<ProgressDemo />

## the checked item waits before it leaves

A checked reminder stays for a moment before it leaves, long enough to see what
you hit and undo it.

<RemindersDemo />

## the label is a slider

In Figma, drag the label beside a number field to scrub its value. The field
still takes typed numbers.

<ScrubDemo />

## the caret glides

In Word, the caret slides to its next position instead of jumping, so your eye
never loses it.

<CaretDemo />

## the page steps back behind a sheet

When an iOS sheet opens, the page behind shrinks and rounds its corners. Drag
the sheet down and the page follows your finger back.

<SheetDemo />

## the copy icon turns into a check

On GitHub, the copy icon becomes a check for two seconds, right where you were
looking, and the button keeps its size.

<CopyDemo />
